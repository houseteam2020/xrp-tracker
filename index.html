What's actually confirmed this week

  • XRP is about $0.96 early Sunday, down about 2.6% over 24 hours and about 17% lower than 7 days ago (CoinGecko snapshot; $0.9640 last trade on Kraken, Kraken 24h range $0.8911 to $0.9974). On Saturday it fell about 10%, its largest one-day percentage loss since Feb 5 (Investing.com), and hit about $0.89, its lowest since November 2024 (CoinDesk). Saturday's close of about $0.91 (CoinGecko) is the lowest daily close since Nov 15, 2024, and below the August 2026 low of about $0.985 (CryptoCompass, Bitstamp data). It has since bounced to about $0.96. 24/7 Wall St found no Ripple-specific news behind the drop; the drivers it documented are market-wide.
  • Bitcoin fell below $64,000 on Saturday and touched a three-month low just above $62,000 before recovering to about $65,500; it is down about 17% over the week and heading for a sixth straight weekly loss (CoinDesk, Investing.com). US spot bitcoin ETFs have had seven straight sessions of outflows since Nov 5, about $1.7B to $1.9B (Farside via Cointelegraph, SoSoValue via Investing.com). About $1.47B of leveraged positions were liquidated in 24 hours, $1.31B of them longs (CoinGlass via Investing.com). Fed funds futures show about 88.7% odds of a quarter-point hike on Dec 9, and the 10-year Treasury yield was about 5.15% on Nov 13 (24/7 Wall St).
  • US spot XRP ETFs had a net outflow of about $19.1M for the week of Nov 9 to 13, the largest weekly outflow since March 2026 (SoSoValue via CoinDesk). Friday Nov 13 was −$1.09M (Franklin's XRPZ +$4.45M, Grayscale's GXRP −$5.54M). The last day with net inflows was Oct 29; net assets are about $1.15B and the funds hold about 1.16B to 1.19B XRP a year after the first one launched on Nov 13, 2025 (Bitcoin.com News, TokenPost). ETFs do not trade on weekends, so Saturday's drop is not in these figures yet.
  • XRP futures open interest has fallen to about $2.21B, a multi-month low, down from about $3.7B in September, and the Crypto Fear and Greed Index is at 18, 'extreme fear' (Benzinga, Nov 14). Lower open interest means less leverage in the market, not necessarily less selling.
  • On the XRP Ledger, the proposed native lending amendment has 22 of 35 trusted-validator votes and the SingleAssetVault amendment it depends on has 23; each needs 29 held for two straight weeks, so neither is live and there is no activation date (CoinDesk, Nov 12). Separately, XRPL developers will reset Testnet and Devnet from 8 to 9 p.m. ET on Nov 25 after a server failure, wiping test accounts; the main XRP Ledger, real XRP balances and transaction history are not affected (TokenPost, Nov 14).
  • Binance has reopened withdrawals on 87 networks, including LTC and NEAR, and says it plans to restore all remaining withdrawal services before next weekend (TokenPost, Nov 13). XRP withdrawals were not among the paused tokens in its latest list.
  • Ripple's Oct 27 partnerships still stand but are mostly plans: BNY now supports XRP and RLUSD custody, while direct RLUSD mint/redeem at BNY is subject to approvals (The Block, Cointelegraph). Robinhood plans to use RLUSD on the XRP Ledger for institutional settlement through Bitstamp and to add RLUSD to its app later in 2026, with no start date or volume announced (Ripple, CryptoSlate).
  • At Ripple's Swell 2026 event, CEO Brad Garlinghouse said Ripple's large XRP holdings do not give it control of the XRP Ledger: Ripple runs one of the 35 validators on the recommended list, and holding XRP carries no voting weight (DailyCoin, Nov 10). Ripple CTO Emeritus David Schwartz disclosed on Nov 9 and 10 that his own holdings are down from about 26M XRP to about 3M over years of pre-arranged sales (crypto.news); that is a personal disclosure, not a Ripple sale.
  • Ripple's November escrow cycle is complete: 1 billion XRP was released at 00:00 UTC Nov 1, and Ripple re-locked 700 million XRP in new escrows, leaving 300 million XRP outside escrow, the same pattern as September and October (TokenPost, Coinpaper, Whale Alert). That does not show the 300 million were sold. The next release is due Dec 1.

How to read the labels

LabelMeaning
OfficialPublished by Ripple, a partner company, or a regulator about itself.
MainstreamGeneral financial press such as Reuters or Bloomberg.
Crypto pressEstablished crypto newsrooms such as CoinDesk, The Block, CryptoSlate.
CommunityCommentators, influencers and post summaries on X, blogs or video.
RumorA claim of fact or future event with no named source.
HighPrimary document, or a newsroom report with named sources or data.
MediumCredible outlet relaying third-party data or company plans not yet delivered.
LowPartly true, but the conclusion drawn goes beyond the evidence.
UnverifiedNo corroboration found. Treat as opinion.
ContradictedAvailable evidence points the other way.
