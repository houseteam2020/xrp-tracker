What's actually confirmed this week

  • XRP is about $1.16 early Sunday, down about 0.7% over 24 hours, about 7.1% lower than 7 days ago and about 17% below 30 days ago (CoinGecko snapshot, 3:21 AM MST; Kraken last trade $1.1597, 24h range $1.1489 to $1.1722). Trading was quiet overnight after Friday's slide to a seven-week low near $1.15; futures open interest fell about 12% as leveraged positions were cut (CoinGlass via BigGo Finance). No Ripple- or XRP Ledger-specific news was behind the move.
  • The US added 165,000 jobs in October, far above the roughly 59,000 to 65,000 economists expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2%; September was revised up to 60,000 (BLS via CoinDesk, Bitcoin Magazine). Bitcoin barely moved on the release, holding near $75,500 to $75,800, but rate-hike odds for the Fed's Dec 9 meeting rose to about 79% (CME FedWatch via Bitcoin Magazine). Thursday's tech-stock sell-off had already pushed crypto lower.
  • Binance is targeting Fri Nov 13 to reopen withdrawals for its last six paused tokens (HYPE, INJ, APT, PLASMA, NEAR, LTC) and all tokens on already-restored networks; no exact day or hour has been given (Binance Recovery Update #9 via MABOnChain, The Coin Republic). XRP withdrawals on the XRP Ledger reopened Nov 3 and XRP on Ethereum on Nov 6. Binance says about 98% of hacked assets are replenished, and the zero-knowledge proof for its Nov 4 Proof of Reserves snapshot is ready for user verification.
  • US spot XRP ETFs went the whole week of Nov 2 to 6 without a single day of net inflows: about $530K left Canary's XRPC on Monday, then four flat days (SoSoValue via Bitcoin.com News). October inflows were about $18.8M, down from $121.4M in September. Cumulative net inflows are about $1.81B; net assets fell to about $1.34B as the price dropped.
  • The XRP Ledger's fixCleanup3_4_0 security amendment has been live since Nov 4 (14:52 UTC); it closed a token-permission flaw affecting issued tokens like stablecoins, not native XRP, and no exploitation was reported. Separately, the proposed native LendingProtocol amendment had 21 of 35 validator votes on Nov 6, short of the 29 needed to start its two-week activation countdown (The Crypto Basic via AD HOC NEWS). It is still a proposal, not a live feature.
  • The XRPL Foundation published a 2027 roadmap listing 24 protocol upgrades (lending, perpetual futures, smart contracts and more), with $5.08M to $5.74M in estimated review and audit costs. Only two are approved; 22 are proposals with no guaranteed launch dates (Coinpaper, Nov 7). An experimental perpetual-futures branch with RLUSD collateral exists but is not an approved upgrade.
  • Exchange data is mixed: Bitcoin.com News reports 418.5M XRP left Binance on Nov 4, its biggest outflow day in nine months (CryptoQuant analyst Darkfost), while CryptoQuant's reserve chart showed Binance's XRP reserves rising from about 2.35B to 2.63B between Nov 3 and 5. Exchange flows can reflect internal wallet moves during Binance's recovery, so neither figure is a clear signal of buying or selling.
  • BNY now supports XRP and RLUSD custody: Ripple and BNY announced Oct 27 that BNY's Digital Asset Custody platform now supports XRP and RLUSD for institutional clients. Plans to let BNY clients mint and redeem RLUSD directly, and a tokenized trade finance pilot with C2FO on the XRP Ledger, are still subject to approvals (The Block, Cointelegraph).
  • Ripple's November escrow cycle is complete: 1 billion XRP was released at 00:00 UTC Nov 1, and Ripple re-locked 700 million XRP in three new escrows that open in June and July 2029, leaving 300 million XRP outside escrow, the same pattern as September and October (TokenPost, Coinpaper, Whale Alert). That does not show the 300 million were sold.

How to read the labels

LabelMeaning
OfficialPublished by Ripple, a partner company, or a regulator about itself.
MainstreamGeneral financial press such as Reuters or Bloomberg.
Crypto pressEstablished crypto newsrooms such as CoinDesk, The Block, CryptoSlate.
CommunityCommentators, influencers and post summaries on X, blogs or video.
RumorA claim of fact or future event with no named source.
HighPrimary document, or a newsroom report with named sources or data.
MediumCredible outlet relaying third-party data or company plans not yet delivered.
LowPartly true, but the conclusion drawn goes beyond the evidence.
UnverifiedNo corroboration found. Treat as opinion.
ContradictedAvailable evidence points the other way.
